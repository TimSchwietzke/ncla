<Statement>

Given `n` non-negative integers describing an elevation map where every bar has width 1, compute how
much rain water it traps.

Water only rests where something taller stands on **both** sides, and it can never rise above the
lower of those two walls. LeetCode shows this as a picture; the wording here has to stand in for it.

<Examples>
  <Example input={`height = [0, 1, 0, 2, 1, 0, 1, 3, 2, 1, 2, 1]`} output="6">
    Six separate units of water, in the dips at indices 2, 4, 5, 6 and 9.
  </Example>
  <Example input={`height = [4, 2, 0, 3, 2, 5]`} output="9">
    One large basin between the 4 on the left and the 5 on the right.
  </Example>
  <Example input={`height = [3, 0, 3]`} output="3">
    A single pit between two equal walls holds three units.
  </Example>
</Examples>

<Constraints>

- `n == height.length`
- `1 <= n <= 2 * 10^4`
- `0 <= height[i] <= 10^5`

</Constraints>

</Statement>

<Signals>

- The water above one position depends on the **maximum to its left** and the **maximum to its
  right** — a per-index quantity split into a left part and a right part. That is prefix/suffix
  thinking before it is two-pointer thinking.
- Those maxima are *running* values: moving one step changes each by at most one comparison. Anything
  that updates in `O(1)` per step wants to be carried, not recomputed.
- Only the **smaller** of the two maxima ever appears in the answer. Half of what you would compute
  is guaranteed to be discarded, which is the opening for dropping the arrays entirely.

</Signals>

<BruteForce>

For every position, scan left for the tallest bar, scan right for the tallest bar, and add
`min(left_max, right_max) - height[i]`.

**O(n²) time, O(1) space.** Correct and easy to argue, which makes it a good thing to say out loud
first. The bottleneck is that both scans are almost the same work each time: the left maximum at
`i + 1` is the left maximum at `i` compared against one new bar. Every position rebuilds a value that
had already been computed one step earlier.

</BruteForce>

<Insight>

The water over a position is set by the smaller of the two surrounding maxima, so the side whose running maximum is smaller already knows its answer.

</Insight>

<Approach>

1. The honest intermediate step is two arrays: `left_max[i]` from a forward pass, `right_max[i]` from
   a backward pass, then sum `min(left_max[i], right_max[i]) - height[i]`. That is **O(n) time and
   O(n) space**, and it is a complete answer worth writing if the pointer version does not come.
2. Now compress it. Put `left` and `right` at the ends, carrying `left_max` and `right_max` for the
   parts already seen.
3. If `left_max < right_max`, the left side is the binding constraint: whatever lies further right,
   there is already something at least `right_max` tall over there, so position `left` can be settled
   now. Step `left` inwards, update `left_max`, and add `left_max - height[left]`.
4. Otherwise do the mirror image on the right.
5. Each step settles exactly one position, so the whole sweep is one pass with two variables.

Updating the maximum **before** adding the water is what keeps the contribution from going negative:
after the update, `left_max` is at least `height[left]`.

</Approach>

<Solution variant="brute">

```python
class Solution:
    def trap(self, height: list[int]) -> int:
        # O(n^2) time, O(1) space
        water = 0

        for i in range(len(height)):
            left_max = 0
            for j in range(i + 1):              # inclusive: the bar itself counts
                left_max = max(left_max, height[j])

            right_max = 0
            for j in range(i, len(height)):
                right_max = max(right_max, height[j])

            water += min(left_max, right_max) - height[i]

        return water
```

</Solution>

<Solution variant="optimal">

```python
class Solution:
    def trap(self, height: list[int]) -> int:
        # O(n) time, O(1) space
        left, right = 0, len(height) - 1
        left_max, right_max = height[left], height[right]
        water = 0

        while left < right:
            if left_max < right_max:            # the left wall is the binding one
                left += 1
                left_max = max(left_max, height[left])
                water += left_max - height[left]
            else:
                right -= 1
                right_max = max(right_max, height[right])
                water += right_max - height[right]

        return water
```

</Solution>

<Pitfalls>

- **Both maxima are inclusive of the position itself.** That is what makes
  `min(left_max, right_max) - height[i]` non-negative. Treat them as strictly left and strictly right
  and a bar taller than everything around it contributes a negative amount.
- **Update the running maximum before adding the water**, not after. Reversing those two lines is the
  bug that produces a negative total on a rising slope, and it survives the first example.
- Move on the comparison of the **maxima**, not of the current bars — or at least know why the two
  formulations agree. The side with the smaller maximum is the one whose level is already pinned;
  the other side is guaranteed to have something at least that tall waiting.
- The two-array version is not a failure. `O(n)` space is a real answer, and it is the right thing to
  write down while you work out the pointer version out loud. Do not skip it and then get stuck.
- This is **not** Container With Most Water. There, two walls hold one body of water and the bars
  between them are ignored; here every position holds its own column and those bars displace water.

</Pitfalls>

<FollowUps>

- *"Solve it with a stack."* — A monotonic decreasing stack of indices fills the basins horizontally,
  layer by layer, in `O(n)` time and `O(n)` space. It is the other pattern LeetCode tags this problem
  with, and it generalises to problems where you need the basins themselves rather than the total.
- *"Return the water above each position, not the sum."* — The two-array version gives it directly.
  The pointer version can too, since each step settles exactly one index — write it there.
- *"Two dimensions."* — LC 407: the boundary is no longer two walls but a whole rim, so you process
  cells in increasing height order out of a min-heap. Same idea, different frontier.

</FollowUps>
